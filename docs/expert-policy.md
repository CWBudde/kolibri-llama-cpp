# Expert cache policies, replayed online

Generated by `go run ./cmd/kolibri-policy -models Kolibri-1-IQ3_XXS-IQ4_XS-down-imx,Kolibri-1-Q8_0 -md docs/expert-policy.md` from the selection files of `tools/locality/experts.py`, the answers in `testdata/locality/<workload>.json` and the expert sizes in `testdata/locality/expert-bytes.json`; do not edit by hand.

The workloads are replayed in the order an online server with a prompt cache meets them: each prompt's new tokens prefilled in ubatches of 512, each answer decoded token by token. A decoded token needs its six experts per layer; a prefill ubatch the union of its tokens' experts, once. The policy learns a step's experts only when the step runs (PLAN Phase 10, Stage A). An answer that stopped at the token limit decodes all but its last token, which the next prompt's prefill processes. Every workload ends with an answer: no request follows it, so the tokens after its decoded ones are never processed online.

Prefill and decode are counted apart: a prefill hit serves a ubatch of up to hundreds of teacher-forced tokens at once and is no decode speedup. MB/token are the expert bytes a phase's misses load per token of that phase. Set recall is the share of steps that found every expert they need resident: a decoded token's exact Top-6 set in a layer, a ubatch's whole union.

Every policy holds at most the same budget: the bytes of the given number of experts of every layer.

- **layer LRU:** an LRU cache of that many experts in every layer, [expert-cache.md](expert-cache.md)'s per-layer policy;
- **shared LRU:** one LRU cache of the whole budget for all layers, its shared policy;
- **LFU:** shared, the experts used least often evicted (one use per step, counts kept for evicted experts too), the least recent of equal counts; all counts are halved every 512 tokens. The half-life is a declared default, not tuned: a value tuned on these traces would be tuned on the test data;
- **ARC:** shared, the Adaptive Replacement Cache (Megiddo and Modha 2003), which has no parameters;
- **oracle †:** Belady's MIN, per layer and shared: it knows every future step and drops the expert needed furthest ahead, the incoming one included. **It uses future knowledge and is an unattainable upper bound, not a policy a server can run.** With equal expert sizes no cache of the budget, prefetching or not, loads fewer bytes.

**Estimated time (an assumption, not a measurement).** Each load, missed or prefetched, goes over one link at a constant bandwidth, one after another: ssd 5 GB/s, pcie 25 GB/s, ram 100 GB/s, each estimated on its own. A step computes once all its experts have arrived, for the time the fully resident model takes on this Mac (`llama-bench` on Metal, [real-checkpoint.md](real-checkpoint.md)): 63.7 tokens/s decode, 1347/s prefill, 15.7 ms per decoded token. Until its experts arrive, it stalls. None of the baselines prefetches, so all their loads stall.

**Held-out splits.** Each workload is split chronologically after one of its answers (`testdata/locality/splits.json`): chronological, per workload: train on the first floor(n/2) of its n answers, up to the last one's decoded tokens; test from the next request's prefill on. Tuning sees training prefixes only, leave-one-workload-out the other workloads' training prefixes. A test suffix is replayed warm, after its prefix, with whatever state the prefix's causal replay left behind, and only its own steps are counted. A policy at test time knows only the steps before it; the answers fix only the order of the spans. The oracle † is still built from the whole trace.

## LFU half-life, tuned on training prefixes

LFU's half-life is its only parameter. At every budget it is chosen from 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192 tokens as the one whose decode misses load the fewest bytes in a training prefix, the first of equal ones, and scored on the test suffix. It is chosen four ways:

- **own prefix:** the workload's own training prefix, chronologically before its test suffix;
- **leave one workload out:** the other workloads' training prefixes, of the same model;
- **other model:** the same workload's training prefix in the other model's trace. The two quantizations route the same tokens differently ([expert-locality.md](expert-locality.md) gives the share of the same expert sets), and their traces are never pooled;
- **hindsight †:** the test suffix itself. **It is tuned on the test data, the bound of tuning and not a result.**

Cells are the half-life and the test suffix's decode MB/token with it; untuned is the declared 512.

## Layer budgets, allocated on training prefixes

A layer LRU need not give every layer the same number of experts. At every budget, the experts per layer are allocated from each layer's miss curve, its decode miss bytes at every capacity in a training prefix, replayed online. The allocation grows the layer whose next step saves the most bytes per byte, along each curve's lower convex hull, until no step saves anything or fits; budget left over is spread level. The capacities are then frozen and the layer LRU replayed online; the cells are its decode MB/token in the test suffixes. Fitted on:

- **own prefix:** the workload's own training prefix;
- **leave one workload out:** the other workloads' training prefixes of the same model, their curves added up;
- **hindsight †:** the test suffix itself. **It is fitted on the test data, the bound of fitting and not a result.**

**Compulsory** are the bytes of a test suffix's first uses of experts, which no cache that only loads what a step needs avoids. Uniform is the layer LRU, shared the shared LRU, at the same budget.

## Stage A gate

A causal policy is retained if, on the decode of all test suffixes of a model together, it beats the shared LRU at one or more budgets: it loads fewer expert bytes, missed or prefetched, or its estimated time per token is lower on a declared link. Equal is no win, and times within a part in 10⁹ are equal. Tuned and allocated policies use the parameter chosen on training prefixes; the oracle and the hindsight fits are bounds and not judged. A size alone under faster means faster on every link.

## Stage B: the decayed-count Markov predictor

The predictor ranks a layer's experts for a step that has not run yet, from steps that have. Per layer it keeps every expert's use count, halved every half-life tokens, and counts how often one decoded set of experts followed another: in the same layer from one token to the next, and within a token from a layer to the layer after next. An expert's score is mix × its share of the transitions out of the latest known set, plus (1 − mix) × its share of the decayed counts; the k best are the candidates, the lower expert first of equal scores. It learns a step's experts only after the step has run, prefill ubatches' uses included, and predicts decoded steps only, from decoded sets. Two horizons, each predicted on its own:

- **next token:** before layer l of token t it predicts layer l−1 of token t+1, from token t's set in layer l−1, which has just run; before layer 0, layer 49 of token t from token t−1's. The prediction is made 49 steps before the step it is for. It uses only earlier tokens' experts;
- **same token:** before layer l of token t it predicts the token's layer l+1, from its set in layer l−1, 1 step ahead. It also uses the token's own completed layers. Nothing is predicted before layer 0, where no layer of the token has run, nor before the last layer, which no layer follows; so layers 0 and 1 are never predicted.

The parameters are chosen per horizon on the own training prefix, as the most needed experts covered summed over k = 6, 12, 24, 48: half-lives 64, 256, 1024, 4096 tokens with mixes 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, the transitions alone (mix 1) having no half-life. **Counts only** is the best mix 0 on the own prefix, **Markov only** mix 1. **Leaving one workload out**, the other workloads' training prefixes of the same model choose them. ## Stage B gate

The predictor is retained if, on the decode of all test suffixes of a model together, it beats the best Stage A policy at one or more budgets: the fewest bytes and the least time on each link of the shared LRU and every policy judged at Stage A's gate, last-N included, at that budget. Each horizon's predictor is judged once per link, with k tuned for that link on the own prefix, on bytes and on that link's time, its CPU cost included. Equal is no win, and times within a part in 10⁹ are equal. An expert prefetched in the training prefix and first used in the test suffix counts in the test suffix's bytes, as if it had loaded it (carried across the cut, `Counts.CarriedPrefetchBytes`): else a wrong prediction in the training prefix that happens to load an expert the test suffix needs first would count as a saving. No Stage A policy carries any; the predictor's time still gains from them, which only flatters it.
